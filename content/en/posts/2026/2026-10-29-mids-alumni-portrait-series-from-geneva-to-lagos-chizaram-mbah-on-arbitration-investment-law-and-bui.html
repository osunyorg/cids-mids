For Chizaram Mbah, the MIDS experience was about more than developing expertise in international dispute settlement. It was also an opportunity to engage with diverse legal traditions, explore new areas of law, and build a broader perspective on how international disputes are resolved. Following experiences at LALIVE, ICSID, and Three Crowns, Chizaram returned to Nigeria, where she is now a Senior Associate in the Dispute Resolution Practice at Aluko & Oyebode. She reflects on the intellectual curiosity, professional opportunities, and international outlook that continue to influence his work today.

body: >-


"The paradox of the MIDS is that it grows your
expertise and confidence in international dispute resolution, yet leaves
you with a greater sense of intellectual humility."
Chizaram Mbah
Senior Associate at Aluko & Oyebode, Lagos, Nigeria

What motivated you to join the MIDS program, and what were your expectations at the time?

I
joined the MIDS programme because I was interested in pursuing a career in
international dispute settlement. I had learnt about the MIDS when I stumbled upon
an article on an issue in international arbitration written by a MIDS alumnus.
When I felt the time was right to pursue a master's degree, the MIDS was one of
my top choices. My expectation at the time was that, through my studies, I
would gain a deeper understanding of international arbitration and the broader
system within which it operates. The MIDS delivered on my expectations.

Which aspect of the MIDS experience had the greatest impact on your professional or personal growth?

The
intensive and special courses on international arbitration and investment law
have had the greatest impact on my professional growth. I studied topics such
as public policy, the arbitration agreement, the New York Convention, multiple
proceedings, and substantive treaty protections, many of which I now encounter
in my international arbitration practice. The rigorous academic training gave
me a solid foundation and the confidence to engage with these issues in
whatever form they take in practice.

How has the MIDS network or training influenced your career path since graduation?

Studying
at the MIDS introduced me to public international law and international
investment law, areas I previously had very little exposure to. I took a keen
interest in those areas, so much so that my thesis focused on examining the
regime for the settlement of intra-African investment disputes. My career has
increasingly tilted towards these fields, and I am now involved in
policy-related work in investment law, as well as in investor-State dispute
resolution.


"As my knowledge expanded and my perspectives were challenged, I became more aware of the limits of my knowledge."
Chizaram Mbah
Senior Associate at Aluko & Oyebode, Lagos, Nigeria

Can you briefly explain your career path since graduating from the MIDS?

After
graduating from the MIDS, I had the opportunity to intern at LALIVE in Geneva,
the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) in
Washington, D.C., and Three Crowns LLP in London. I then returned to my home
country, Nigeria, and joined Aluko & Oyebode, a large full-service law firm
in Lagos, where I currently work as a Senior Associate in the Dispute
Resolution practice.

If you could redesign one aspect of international dispute resolution, what would it be and why?

I would
redesign the geographic spread of international dispute resolution, so that
less-established jurisdictions have greater opportunities to develop the
capacity to handle complex international disputes. International disputes are
often governed by the laws of, or resolved in, traditional centres in Europe
and the United States. I recognise that this concentration largely reflects
party autonomy and the legitimacy that these centres have built over many years.
I would therefore support initiatives that strengthen the institutions of
less-established jurisdictions and build confidence in their dispute resolution
systems.

What advice would you give to future MIDS students embarking on this journey?

Don't
have tunnel vision. Everyone joins the MIDS with a goal, whether it is to build
expertise in a particular area of international dispute settlement, launch a
career in the field, or grow a professional and personal network. Stay focused
on your goal, but don't overlook the other opportunities the MIDS offers. Keep
an open mind and engage with every aspect of the programme. You never know what
might spark your curiosity and shape your career path.
