Gracious Avayiwoe
was the first Ghanaian admitted to the Master in International Dispute
Settlement (MIDS). Recipient of the LALIVE Scholarship, he explains how the
programme contributed to his academic development and helped lay the
foundations for a career in international law, leading to professional
experiences at some of the world's leading international institutions.

A scholarship that made a difference

Having already
completed an LL.M. in substantive international law, Gracious wanted to deepen
his understanding of its procedural dimensions. The MIDS offered precisely the
specialised training he was looking for, while Geneva's position as a centre of
international law and dispute settlement made it an especially attractive
destination.

However, the
opportunity to pursue the programme was made possible by the LALIVE
Scholarship. Covering his tuition fees and providing a monthly stipend, the
scholarship allowed him to focus entirely on his studies.

As he explains, the
financial support made the MIDS an important part of his scholarly and
professional development, enabling him to acquire the deeper understanding of
international law he had hoped for.

Your support did
more than fund my studies; it helped strengthen the foundation for my career in
international law. I am deeply grateful.

Gracious Avayiwoe
MIDS 2021 | LALIVE Scholarship Recipient

From the MIDS to international institutions

After completing
the programme, Gracious joined the World Trade Organization (WTO) as the first
Ghanaian in its Accession Internship Programme.

He subsequently
undertook further fellowships at the United Nations Conference on Trade and
Development (UNCTAD), the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS)
and the International Court of Justice (ICJ).

These experiences
allowed him to continue exploring the fields of international law and dispute
settlement that had first shaped his academic interests during the MIDS.

Continuing his research at the University of Hong Kong

Gracious is now
approaching the completion of his PhD in Law at the University of Hong Kong
(HKU).

His doctoral
research focuses on public international law and international dispute
settlement, examining the limits of consistency in treaty interpretation at the
International Court of Justice. The project builds on his MIDS dissertation on
the Court, which was subsequently published in the Journal of International
Dispute Settlement .

He is also
developing his doctoral research into a book, drawing on perspectives gained
through further research experiences, including a fellowship at the University
of Cambridge.

A lasting impact

Reflecting on the
impact of the LALIVE Scholarship, Gracious chooses not to summarise it in a
sentence, but in a single word: "formative."

He explains that
the donors' support did more than finance his studies: it helped strengthen the
foundations of his career in international law. His experience
illustrates how financial support at a decisive moment can contribute to a
lasting academic and professional trajectory, opening opportunities that extend
well beyond the duration of a degree.
